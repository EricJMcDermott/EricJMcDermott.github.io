# Portfolio audit · 9 October 2026

**Status:** The editorial direction below was rejected by Eric. The current implementation is the immersive studio documented in DESIGN.md. The content findings remain useful, but the “working atlas” visual recommendation is superseded.

## The central problem

The existing site communicates curiosity and range, but the professional story is buried in a PDF. A visitor meets a torus, broad categories, and personal interests before learning that Eric leads AI R&D, builds commercial products, and has a PhD in neuroscience. The design is memorable at the level of a visual effect; the work needs to be memorable too.

## Evidence and positioning

Primary source: science/EJM-CV.pdf, the current two-page CV in this repository. It identifies Eric as Principal Specialist AI at diconium, Founder and Head of Product of Meuccitech, and creator of BabyBaby. It describes hands-on engineering as well as product strategy and cross-functional leadership. The file is already publicly linked by the site; its claims are self-reported rather than independently audited.

Working position: **AI product and technology leader who connects research, engineering, and commercial reality.** The audience question is pending; this position follows the current CV, not an invented job-search status.

The professional promise is the ability to turn an uncertain opportunity into a working system and help a team carry it into use. Personal work supplies texture and evidence of observation, experimentation, and finishing things. It should not have to carry the entire hiring argument.

## Reference research

- [Taste](https://github.com/Leonxlnx/taste-skill): a design skill collection rather than a runtime library. Useful for deliberate composition, varied visual rhythm, and auditing an existing interface before replacing it.
- [Impeccable](https://impeccable.style/): design guidance and critique tools. The useful lesson is to connect hierarchy, typography, interaction, and clear language rather than accumulating effects.
- [Awesome Design MD](https://github.com/VoltAgent/awesome-design-md): examples of coherent design systems. Used as a reminder to define a small visual system; no brand template has been copied.
- [Awesome Design](https://github.com/gztchan/awesome-design): a wider resource index. The name is ambiguous; several projects use it.
- [21st.dev](https://21st.dev/): component inspiration. This may be the intended “Image 23.js,” but that identification is unconfirmed. It is not listed as a dependency or installed tool.
- [Lusion Labs](https://lusion.co/projects/lusion_labs/): the portfolio demonstrates the technical practice it describes. Apply this principle through useful project exploration and explanatory diagrams.
- [Bruno Simon](https://bruno-simon.com/): a site can be a demonstration of its author's work. Eric's equivalent should show connections and decisions; a driving game would demonstrate a different specialty.
- [Diconium's Dicobot interview](https://special.diconium.com/en/dicobot-ai-chatbot/alpha): public first-party evidence of Eric's involvement in enterprise AI product development. Link it directly from the AI project brief.

These references were researched, not installed or blindly imported. The implementation remains static HTML/CSS/JavaScript on GitHub Pages. The site gains a bespoke interactive diagram without requiring a new framework, tracking, or a GPU-heavy opening.

## Visual thesis: a working atlas

An editorial field notebook meets an engineering diagram. Warm paper, dark ink, restrained vermilion, precise rules, large type, and real artifacts. The project map is the distinctive opening interaction: it makes the relationship between research, AI systems, products, and making visible. Motion traces those relationships and responds to a real link. Native scrolling, readable content, and ordinary links are always available.

## Content boundaries and open questions

1. Which opportunities matter most: AI product leadership, technical leadership, consulting, or research translation? Awaiting Eric's direction.
2. Stronger case studies need one hard decision per project, alternatives considered, and a lesson from something that did not work. Those stories cannot be inferred from the CV.
3. Client work would benefit from approved screenshots, an architecture excerpt, and a clearly bounded before/after measurement. No fabricated dashboard or confidential artifact is used.
4. Outcome metrics from the CV need time windows, denominators, and individual-versus-team attribution before stronger causal claims. The new copy preserves their reported scope and links to the CV.
5. A short first-person introduction or portrait could add warmth. No stock portrait or invented biography is substituted.
6. Testimonials would help if named people explicitly permit publication. None are invented.
